Craft

How I think about product

Six principles that show up in every piece of work I do.

Clarity before code

Ambiguous requirements cost 10x more after development starts. The spec is the product until something ships.

Edge cases first

Most user pain lives in error states, empty states, and the unhappy path. I design those before I design the happy path.

Promise equals proof

If the UI promises something, "free cancellation", "confirmed", "synced", the system must visibly back it up.

Prototype to decide

Teams decide better reacting to something concrete than debating abstractions. Build the thing that ends the argument.

Honest scope

I say what I did and what the developers did. Overclaiming destroys trust faster than any missed deadline.

Numbers over adjectives

Every claim should carry a metric or an artifact. "Improved" means nothing. A before and after means something.

Approach

How I plug in

Three phases, whether I'm joining as product lead, BA, or early-team operator.

01

Listen where it hurts

Talk to users and stakeholders, map current workflows, push past the presented problem to find the actual one. Agree on the real problem before touching a solution.

02

Shape and prototype

User flows, wireframes, requirements, and AI-assisted prototypes at the right fidelity. Teams react better to something concrete than to a deck full of abstractions.

03

Build, verify, refine

Work with developers through build, clarify edge cases in real time, define acceptance criteria, and learn from actual usage. Shipping is the beginning, not the end.
