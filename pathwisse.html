Pathwisse is a B2B student success platform built at Shaquantum Labs. It connects academic management, career development and placement preparation in a single system used by students, faculty, heads of departments and institutional management.

Discovery started on-site, not in a spreadsheet. The team visited five colleges, met over 160 stakeholders across all roles, and built the platform to solve the problems that actually surfaced in those conversations.

03

The Fragmented Student-Success Problem

Placement readiness was treated as a final-year problem, not a continuous process.

Every role in the institution had a version of the same problem: no unified view, no early signals, no connected workflow between academics and career preparation.

Role
Pain point
Students
No structured guidance from academics to placement readiness. Practice was self-managed, career planning was reactive and came too late.
Faculty
No single view of which students were progressing or falling behind. Tracking was manual, visibility was limited, and intervention came after the damage.
HODs
No department-wide aggregate without manual collation across spreadsheets and individual reports. No ability to spot batch-level trends early.
Management
No institutional dashboard. Placement readiness data existed in silos by department with no cross-college or cross-department view.

The gap was structural: the same student's academic record, career readiness practice, and placement eligibility lived in completely separate systems with no connection between them.

05

Research Insights

Students need structure, not just access

Access to practice material was not the problem. Students needed a guided roadmap that connected their current academic stage to a concrete path toward placement readiness.

Faculty want early signals, not end-of-term reports

By the time placement season started, it was too late to intervene meaningfully. Faculty needed visibility into student progress throughout the academic cycle — not just at the end.

HODs need aggregates, not individual records

HODs did not want to track individual students. They needed department-wide and batch-level summaries that let them identify systemic gaps without reviewing case by case.

Management needed cross-college visibility

Institutional management could not compare placement readiness across departments or colleges. Every report was produced on demand, manually.

Readiness is a process, not an event

Across all five colleges and all four roles, the common theme was the same: placement readiness was treated as something to address in the final year, not built continuously from year one.

Communication skills were consistently underserved

Aptitude preparation had workarounds — coaching classes, practice papers. Communication and interview skills had almost none. That gap was the strongest signal for Practice Labs.

Product Decision & Roadmap Prioritisation

Roadmap decisions were shared with the core team. I contributed problem synthesis and requirements; strategy and commercial positioning were team decisions.

The four-portal architecture was the foundational decision. Every feature that followed was built on top of role-based access and a shared data layer.

Priority
Decision and rationale
Foundation
Build all four portals with role-based permissions first. Without the institutional hierarchy in place, individual features would have no context to sit in.
Early high-value feature
Practice Labs — aptitude and communication practice. It was the most consistently requested feature across all five colleges and was demonstrable quickly, making it the right feature to lead with.
Differentiated value
Career roadmaps and personalised learning. This was what separated Pathwisse from a generic practice tool — the connection between where a student is and where they need to go.

AI mock-interview voice agent. High effort, high signal — built after the core platform was validated so the team could invest in it with confidence.

Opportunities layer — connecting placement-ready students to relevant opportunities. Planned and scoped; execution depends on partner institutional relationships.
07

Product Architecture

Pathwisse is structured around a shared data layer that connects all four portals. A student's activity in the student portal becomes visible to their assigned faculty, aggregated for the HOD, and rolled up for management — each role seeing only what is relevant and appropriate for their function.

Role-based access and permissions

Each portal is scoped to the role. A faculty member sees only the students assigned to them. An HOD sees their department. Management sees across departments and colleges. Access control was specified in requirements before any implementation began.

Connected data flow

Student activity → Faculty tracking view → HOD department rollup → Management institutional report. Data flows upward by role without any manual export or report generation step.

Deep Dive: Tracking & Intervention

The most consistent request from faculty was not a feature — it was a timing problem. They wanted to know about struggling students before placement season, not during.

The tracking system was designed to surface two types of signal: engagement (is the student actively using the platform?) and progress (are they moving toward readiness targets at the expected pace?). Both matter independently. A student can be active but plateauing, or absent but not yet behind their cohort.

Engagement signal

Daily and weekly activity tracked at the individual level. Faculty see recency, frequency and streak information without needing to query the student directly.

Progress signal

Readiness milestones tracked against expected pace for the student's year and career roadmap. Progress that falls below threshold triggers a faculty flag, not a final-year crisis.

Intervention design

Flags are surfaced to the faculty member, not pushed to the student as a system alert. The intervention stays human — faculty decide when and how to act on a flag.

HOD aggregate view

At the department level, the HOD sees the proportion of students at risk, not individual flag lists. Intervention at the HOD level is about patterns, not cases.

Deep Dive: Career Roadmaps & Personalised Learning

Career roadmaps were the feature that separated Pathwisse from a generic practice tool. The question they answer is: given where this student is right now, what do they need to do to become placement-ready in their chosen career path?

Student selects a career path (e.g. software engineering, data analysis, operations management)

Pathwisse maps the gap between the student's current academic stage and the target role's placement requirements

A structured learning progression is generated, covering skill areas the student needs to develop

Practice sessions are assigned in the relevant skill areas, tied to the roadmap rather than generic topic lists

Progress on the roadmap is updated as the student completes practice, so they always know where they stand

Deep Dive: Practice Labs & AI Mock Interviews

Practice Labs addresses the specific gap that all five college visits surfaced: students had some path to aptitude preparation, but almost no structured path to communication and interview readiness.

Aptitude practice

Daily aptitude exercises covering quantitative reasoning, verbal ability, and logical reasoning. Designed for short daily sessions rather than long study blocks, with difficulty adapting based on performance.

Communication practice

Structured exercises for clarity, confidence and structured response. Covers both written and spoken communication, with scenario-based prompts drawn from real placement interview patterns.

AI Mock Interview Voice Agent

The AI mock interview agent is a voice-based system that simulates a real interview conversation. The student speaks their answers; the agent responds with follow-up questions and evaluates clarity, structure and content.

Why voice, not text

The gap students struggled with was not knowing what to say — it was saying it fluently under pressure. Text-based practice does not replicate that. Voice does.

Design decisions I owned

User flow for the interview session, edge cases (connection drop, mic permissions, no-answer handling), feedback display requirements, and acceptance criteria for evaluation quality.

Validation & Onboarding of Four Colleges

Four colleges completed onboarding. The path from discovery to conversion was built on demonstrations to the actual stakeholders we had met during discovery.

Demonstration process

Demonstrations were conducted with HODs and management at target colleges. I owned the demo structure and delivery, walking institutional stakeholders through all four portals, with the interface tailored to the role of the person in the room.

Feedback analysis

Post-demonstration feedback was collected and synthesised into product changes. Objections and feature requests were triaged against the roadmap rather than reacted to immediately.

Pilot planning

Pilot planning was shared with the core team. I contributed onboarding requirements and user flow documentation so the pilot launch had clear success conditions for each role.

UAT

Acceptance testing was run against the acceptance criteria I had written. Issues found during UAT were logged with reproduction steps and priority so engineering could resolve them before institutional rollout.

Current Limitations

Pathwisse is a live, in-use product. These are honest limitations that exist at the point this case study was written.

Opportunities layer is planned, not fully live

The opportunities layer is scoped and designed. Full execution depends on institutional partnerships and commercial agreements that are not yet in place.

AI mock interview is early-stage

The voice agent is live and in use but is in an early stage. Evaluation accuracy and question quality improve with more usage data. The feedback loop between sessions and product improvement is still being established.

Outcome metrics are not yet measured

Placement outcome data (whether students who used Pathwisse placed better) requires a full placement cycle with instrumented tracking. That data does not exist yet and I am not claiming it does.

Personalisation is rule-based, not adaptive

Personalised learning currently follows structured roadmap progressions. True adaptive personalisation — where the system changes in real time based on individual performance patterns — is a future capability, not current.

Product Learnings

Discovery at this scale changes your product

Visiting five colleges and meeting 160+ stakeholders before building anything produced a fundamentally different product than a desk-based assumptions document would have. The four-portal structure, the intervention-first faculty view, and the focus on communication practice were all direct outputs of what we heard on-site.

Role-based design requires role-based research

Every role had a different relationship with the same problem. HODs needed aggregates, not individual records. Faculty needed early signals, not end-of-term summaries. Building the right thing for each role required understanding each role independently before building a system that connected them all.

Acceptance criteria before UAT, not during

Writing acceptance criteria before build — not after — made UAT faster and less ambiguous. Issues found during UAT had a clear definition of "resolved" because the expected behaviour had been specified in advance.

Be honest about what's proposed and what's measured

Pathwisse has not yet produced placement outcome data. That's a real limitation. Claiming otherwise would have been dishonest in demonstrations and would have set expectations that erode trust. Honesty about what's early-stage is not a weakness in a demo — it's credibility.

The best product decision is the one you can defend with what you actually heard, not what you assumed.

Every major product decision on Pathwisse — the four-portal structure, the focus on communication practice, the intervention-first design — was directly traceable to something a real stakeholder said in a real college. That traceability is what I believe makes product work trustworthy.
